# The four systems as Web of Science queries

One query per system and goal, written from the queries their authors
published. Decision D033; contract `docs/wos-sdg-contract.md`. Read-only,
with `SHA256SUMS`. Generated by `sdg_tech_tagger.wos_sdg.build`, never
edited:

```bash
uv run python -c "from sdg_tech_tagger import wos_sdg; print(len(wos_sdg.build('published')))"
```

## What is here

| directory | written from | goals | characters, all goals | largest goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `aurora/` | Aurora v5.0.3, 378 query lines | 17 | 37,839 | 4,392 |
| `elsevier/` | Elsevier 2019 v1 | 16 | 24,305 | 2,173 |
| `auckland/` | Auckland v2.0 | 16 | 83,313 | 7,709 |
| `siris/` | SIRIS vocabulary v1.3, 3,445 rows | 16 | 181,559 | 28,008 |

Each file `SDG-<nn>.txt` is one line, `TS=( ... )`, ready to paste in the
advanced search of the Web of Science. Elsevier, Auckland and SIRIS have
no goal 17. The largest file is `siris/SDG-03.txt`, with 1,297 `OR`.

To use them with another search, run each goal once and combine the
numbers of the search history, for example
`#1 AND (#2 OR #3 OR #4 OR #5)`.

Aurora is written from v5.0.3 and not from the v5.0 that text2sdg and the
port use: Aurora corrected 12 lines with typing errors (D027, D033).

## How a query is written

- `W/n` is `NEAR/n`, `AND NOT` is `NOT`, `TITLE-ABS-KEY` is `TS`.
- Every group has its own parentheses, because Scopus and the Web of
  Science do not bind their operators in the same order.
- Every term is in double quotes, including those in braces and the bare
  words of the published query.
- A SIRIS row is its keyword, or its keyword `AND` its extra terms joined
  by `OR`.

## What is shown, and what is not

Shown, by `tests/unit/test_wos_sdg.py`: each file says what the published
query says, operator for operator. The text read back by the binding
order of the Web of Science gives the tree the published query gives by
the binding order of Scopus.

Run on the Web of Science on 2026-10-11, after these files were written
(`probes/wos_run_probe`): the 65 queries are all accepted and return
records, from 47,077 (`elsevier/SDG-01.txt`) to 17,553,028
(`auckland/SDG-03.txt`). Run once each, they combine through the numbers
of the search history: the whole technology gate `AND` goal 1 of the four
systems joined by `OR` returned 20,904 records, 8,333 for 2023 to 2025.
A "Did you mean?" suggestion is shown for nine of the 65 and was not
followed: it proposes another spelling of one term.

They are not the port: the port follows the matching rules of text2sdg
and says which query fired on which words, and the Web of Science returns
papers. That a query runs says nothing of whether its records are those
the port fires on.

## `NOTES.tsv`: where a query may not mean what it meant in Scopus

One row per place where the text was thought likely to be refused, or may
not mean what it meant in Scopus. 29 of the 65 files have at least one.
None was refused in the run of 2026-10-11: a `NEAR` inside a `NEAR`,
`"*forest*"`, a term with parentheses and a term outside ASCII are all
accepted.

| code | rows | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `chain` | 48, Aurora | `A W/3 B W/3 C`, written `(A NEAR/3 B) AND (B NEAR/3 C)`. Scopus does not document how it reads a chain; this is what text2sdg searches (D017). |
| `nested-proximity` | 25, Aurora | a `NEAR` inside a `NEAR`. Accepted; how the Web of Science counts the distance there is not documented. |
| `left-truncation` | 3 | `"*forest*"`, a wildcard at the start of a word, in Elsevier and Auckland. |
| `braces-wildcard` | 6 | a term in braces with a `*` or `?`, which Scopus reads as a character and which is a wildcard here: `micro-financ*` and `microfinanc*` in Auckland; `discriminatory law*`, `economic marginali?ation`, `pollutant*` and `girl* AND violence` in Elsevier. |
| `non-ascii` | 16 | a term with a letter or a dash outside ASCII, such as `el niño` or `lithium–air battery`. |
| `parenthesis` | 3, SIRIS | a term holding parentheses, such as `sole (fish)`. |

Two published terms are typing errors of their authors and are written
as published: Elsevier's `{girl* AND violence}` and `{women AND violence}`
are phrases holding the word AND, and Auckland has `la niã`.

## Where the files are not Scopus

- Scopus also finds the plural and the spelling variants of a term in
  double quotes or bare. A quoted Web of Science term is searched as
  written, so these files find less than Scopus would there, and read a
  term as the port does.
- `TS` and `TITLE-ABS-KEY` both read title, abstract and keywords, and
  the keyword fields of the two databases are not the same.
